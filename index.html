Clinical-Documentation-Assistant

Miaohua Zhang,
Healthcare Clinic
Canberra

Motivation / Pain Point

The project was motivated by a real documentation challenge in a Chinese medicine clinic. Doctors were spending around 10 minutes manually writing each medical record after consultation. This process was time-consuming and difficult to scale during busy clinic hours.

The workflow also involved several domain-specific challenges. Chinese medicine records contain specialised terms such as herb names, acupoints, dosage expressions, symptoms, and treatment descriptions. These terms are often difficult to type and can be easily misrecognized by general-purpose speech-to-text systems.

In addition, the clinic needed Chinese medical records for internal clinical use, while English records were also useful for communication with other healthcare providers and patients.

The goal of MediTalk was therefore not just to transcribe speech, but to convert noisy spoken clinical input into structured, reviewable, bilingual documentation.

System Workflow

MediTalk follows an end-to-end voice-to-note workflow. The doctor first selects the patient, doctor, and visit date, then records or enters the clinical content. The system supports both real-time speech transcription during recording and a final transcription path after the recording is stopped. The generated clinical content is then refined, structured, validated, reviewed by the doctor, and saved or exported as part of the patient record.

End-to-End User Workflow

The user-facing workflow starts from patient and doctor management, followed by voice input, draft generation, clinical review, and final save/export. The system is designed around the clinic's actual documentation process, where the AI generates a structured draft but the doctor remains in control of the final record.

Step Workflow Stage Description
1 Patient / Doctor Management The doctor selects or manages the patient, doctor profile, and visit information before documentation begins.
2 Voice Input The doctor records clinical content through voice input. The interface supports start/stop recording and real-time transcript display.
3 Speech-to-Text and Correction The transcription workflow applies speech-to-text processing and domain-specific correction for terminology, homophones, and dosage expressions.
4 Clinical Information Extraction The system extracts structured clinical information, such as symptoms, diagnosis, treatment, herbs, acupoints, and clinical advice.
5 Documentation Generation The system generates a readable clinical note and SOAP-style documentation in Chinese and English.
6 Validation and Review A validation agent checks completeness and consistency, and the doctor reviews and edits the draft before approval.
7 Save / Export After doctor approval, the visit record is saved and can be exported or printed for clinical use.

Speech Subflow

The speech workflow contains two paths. The real-time path provides live transcription feedback during recording, while the final path performs a more complete transcription and correction process after the doctor stops recording.

Realtime Path

During recording, the frontend calls the realtime transcription route to display a live transcript. This gives the doctor immediate feedback and supports live correction of terminology, homophones, and dosage expressions.

  • Start recording
  • Call /ws/realtime-transcribe
  • Display live transcript in the UI
  • Apply lightweight live correction

Final Path

After the doctor stops recording, the system runs a more complete transcription and correction pipeline. The final transcript is then used for clinical extraction and draft generation.

  • Stop recording
  • Call /transcribe-audio
  • Use fallback STT when needed
  • Finalize post-ASR correction
  • Call /generate-draft

Backend and Agent Orchestration

The backend is implemented with FastAPI and provides routes for realtime transcription, audio transcription, draft generation, visit saving, and history retrieval. The multi-agent layer coordinates transcription, clinical extraction, documentation generation, and validation. External services include realtime STT, fallback file-based STT, LLM APIs, and local rule assets such as glossary, hotwords, homophone rules, and dosage normalization.

This modular design makes the workflow easier to debug and improve. Each component has a clear responsibility, and the final clinical record is only saved after doctor review.

Knowledge-Grounded ASR Post-Correction

A key technical challenge in MediTalk was that general-purpose speech-to-text systems were not reliable enough for Chinese medicine documentation. The raw transcript could contain errors in herb names, acupoints, dosage expressions, and homophones. These errors were especially important because they could propagate into downstream clinical extraction, bilingual documentation, and SOAP-style note generation.

To improve reliability, I designed a knowledge-grounded ASR post-correction layer. Instead of treating the speech-to-text output as final, the system checks the raw transcript against a domain-specific medical terminology knowledge base. This knowledge base includes symptoms, Chinese herb names, acupoints, treatment terms, dosage normalization rules, hotwords, aliases, homophone rules, and common ASR error mappings.

The correction layer performs candidate term matching, context-aware disambiguation, terminology normalization, and lightweight validation. The output is not only a cleaner transcript, but also a set of terminology-grounded structured hints, such as possible complaint, examination, syndrome, prescription, treatment, and advice fields. These hints are then reused by the structured clinical extraction module to build a richer semantic representation of the visit.

The key design principle is that the LLM is not asked to freely rewrite the doctor’s speech. Instead, the correction process is guided by controlled domain knowledge and clinical context. This makes the downstream documentation workflow more stable, reviewable, and easier to validate.

Figure 2. Knowledge-grounded ASR post-correction layer for domain-specific clinical speech documentation.

Knowledge Sources Used for Correction

Knowledge Source Purpose in Post-Correction Downstream Use
Symptoms and clinical phrases Normalize common symptom descriptions and clinical expressions. Supports complaint, history, and symptom extraction.
Chinese herb names Correct rare or easily misrecognized herb names in the raw transcript. Supports herbal medication and prescription fields.
Acupoints and treatment terms Improve recognition of acupuncture points and treatment-related terminology. Supports therapy and treatment detail extraction.
Dosage normalization rules Standardize dosage expressions and reduce ambiguity in medication instructions. Supports medication review and safer bilingual reporting.
Hotwords, aliases, and homophone rules Handle domain-specific pronunciation variation, aliases, and Chinese homophone errors. Improves terminology consistency before structured extraction.
Common ASR error mappings Map frequently observed speech-to-text mistakes back to plausible clinical terms. Provides terminology-grounded structured hints for the semantic extraction layer.

This design reflects a research-to-practice principle: for domain-specific LLM systems, reliability often comes from combining model capability with external domain structure, controlled terminology, validation rules, fallback mechanisms, and human review.

Structured Clinical Information Extraction

After MediTalk produces a corrected transcript from the speech-to-text and medical terminology post-correction pipeline, the structured extraction module converts free-form clinical dictation into a structured intermediate clinical representation. This layer separates clinical understanding from final documentation generation, so the system can check source support, repair field-level issues, and generate reviewable Chinese notes, English reports, and SOAP-style documentation.

The primary extraction path reuses knowledge-grounded structured hints generated during ASR post-correction. These hints are converted into a richer internal structure called the VisitSemanticFrame, which captures chief complaint, history, objective findings, TCM assessment, treatment principle, therapies, herbal medication, advice, follow-up, source evidence, repair logs, and review issues.

MediTalk also keeps a legacy keyword and rule-based extraction path as a stable fallback. When the semantic extraction path has low confidence, incomplete coverage, or is not suitable for an explicitly sectioned transcript, the system can fall back to a simpler ClinicalExtractionResult schema for downstream documentation. This keeps the workflow robust while allowing the system to use a richer semantic layer when appropriate.

Extraction Workflow

The structured extraction workflow converts the corrected transcript into a richer semantic representation, applies repair and source-evidence checks, and then passes the structured result to downstream clinical documentation generation.

Figure 3. MediTalk structured clinical extraction workflow from corrected transcript to evidence-checked draft notes.

Structured Output Schema

Structured Component What It Captures Why It Matters
VisitSemanticFrame A rich semantic structure for symptoms, history, findings, assessment, treatment, medication, advice, and follow-up. Provides documentation generation with a clinically meaningful intermediate representation.
Knowledge Structured Hints Structured fields reused from the ASR knowledge repair stage, including complaint, exam, syndrome, prescription, and advice hints. Reduces repeated extraction work and starts from terminology-grounded metadata.
Legacy Extraction Keyword and rule-based ClinicalExtractionAgent.extract() output. Provides a stable fallback when the richer semantic path is unavailable or less suitable.
Semantic Repair Moves medication, treatment, assessment, or objective finding text out of incorrect fields. Reduces field contamination before the system writes the clinical note.
Source Evidence Validation Checks whether extracted TCM assessment, herbs, doses, acupoints, advice, and follow-up details are supported by the transcript. Removes or flags unsupported information before downstream report generation.
ClinicalExtractionResult Backward-compatible structured note schema used by the existing documentation and frontend workflow. Keeps downstream interfaces stable while allowing a richer semantic layer internally.
Validation + Coverage Flags Warnings for missing facts, unsupported evidence, field contamination, incomplete medication details, or English report issues. Supports doctor review before the visit record is saved.

Why This Module Is Important

This module is more than a keyword extractor. It combines knowledge reuse, semantic structuring, deterministic repair, source-grounding checks, and fallback design. This makes the generated medical record easier to review, safer to translate, and more reliable for a bilingual clinical documentation workflow.

The same design pattern is transferable to other regulated AI workflows. Instead of asking an LLM to directly write the final answer, the system first creates a structured, reviewable, evidence-aware representation, and then uses that structure for final document generation.

Evaluation Framework and Pilot Results

The MediTalk evaluation framework evaluates the full clinical documentation pipeline through a reproducible offline process. The current pilot evaluation uses 25 clinician-provided recordings with corresponding gold transcripts and structured clinical gold files.

Instead of rerunning models for every evaluation, the framework reads archived system runs and compares them against gold references. This makes the results more stable, auditable, and useful for regression tracking as the system evolves.

The evaluation is intentionally layered. It separately measures transcript quality, structured clinical extraction quality, and final Chinese report quality. This helps locate whether an error comes from ASR, knowledge-grounded post-correction, semantic extraction, or the report generation stage.

A no-postprocessing baseline is also included. It compares raw STT text from audit sidecars with the final postprocessed transcript, so the project can quantify how much the knowledge-grounded repair layer improves character accuracy, herbal term retention, and acupoint recognition.

Evaluation Workflow

The evaluation workflow starts from gold clinical cases and archived system runs, then applies layered metrics, clinical matching, error analysis, cost analysis, and improvement decisions.

Evaluation Layers

The evaluation is divided into multiple layers so that errors can be traced to the specific stage where they occur. This is important for improving a multi-stage LLM workflow, because transcript errors, extraction errors, and final report errors require different fixes.

Evaluation Layer What It Compares Main Metrics What It Diagnoses
Layer 1: Transcript Accuracy transcript_gold.txt vs system/transcript_system.txt CER, character accuracy, herbal term recall, acupoint recall / F1. Whether ASR and post-correction preserve the dictated clinical content.
Layer 2: Structured Extraction structured_gold_v2_knowledge.json vs semantic_frame in draft.json Symptom recall / F1, TCM assessment, herbs + dosages, acupoints, and follow-up components. Whether transcript facts are correctly converted into structured clinical fields.
Layer 3: Final Report Structured gold vs generated Chinese readable note sections. Missing facts, unsupported facts, high-risk omissions, and high-risk additions. Whether clinically important facts survive into the doctor-facing report.
No-Postprocessing Baseline Raw STT audit text vs postprocessed transcript, both compared with gold transcript. Raw vs postprocessed CER, character accuracy, herbal recall, and acupoint recall / F1. How much the knowledge-grounded post-correction module improves raw STT output.
Cost Evaluation cost/cost_summary.json across archived runs. STT cost, knowledge repair cost, Chinese note cost, English translation cost, and full bilingual workflow cost. Helps understand the quality-cost trade-off for future workflow scaling.

Practical Workflow Benefit

In addition to information capture and report quality, MediTalk was also assessed for practical workflow benefit. Manual documentation typically took around 10 minutes per record. With MediTalk, the documentation process was reduced to around 1–2 minutes per record, including doctor review.

Workflow Approximate Time per Record Notes
Manual documentation Around 10 minutes Doctor manually writes the medical record after consultation.
MediTalk workflow Around 1–2 minutes AI generates a structured draft; the doctor reviews, edits, and approves the final record.

Why This Evaluation Design Matters

The evaluation does not hide the system behind a single overall score. By separating transcript accuracy, structured extraction quality, final report accuracy, raw-vs-post ablation, per-case error details, and cost, it gives the team a practical map for deciding what to improve next.

This design is especially important for clinical AI systems. The evaluation tracks not only recall and F1, but also missing facts, unsupported additions, and high-risk omissions such as herbs, dosages, and acupoints. These outputs make the system easier to audit, safer to review, and more suitable for iterative medical documentation development.

The same evaluation mindset is transferable to enterprise AI workflows. In regulated domains such as banking, a reliable GenAI system should be evaluated not only by final answer quality, but also by source grounding, structured extraction accuracy, missing or unsupported facts, human review effort, and operational cost.
